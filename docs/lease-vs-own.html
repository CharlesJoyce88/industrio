When owning makes sense

Five tenant profiles where a condo usually wins — and where it usually doesn't.

✓ Stable, long-tenure owner-operator

5+ year track record, predictable revenue, owner drawing salary through a CCPC. Owning lets you pay rent to yourself via a holding company and build equity.

✓ Customization-heavy operations

Crane rails, heavy power upgrades, fixed racking, specialized ventilation — landlords often won't underwrite these. Ownership removes the approval bottleneck.

✓ Forecastable 7–10 yr space need

You know the footprint will hold. Breakeven on most GTA industrial condo deals lands between years 5 and 8 net of transaction costs.

✓ Estate & wealth planning angle

Real estate outside the operating company can be passed, gifted, or used as security with flexibility that a lease cannot offer.

✗ Rapidly growing business

If you might need 2× the space in 3 years, a condo is a constraint. Leases (or larger buildings with expansion options) usually win.

✗ Capital-starved operation

A 30% down payment plus closing costs is dead capital from an operating standpoint. If your business earns a higher return on reinvested capital, leasing wins.

✗ Short expected hold (<5 yr)

Transaction costs — land transfer tax, legal, inspections, broker fees on exit — usually erase the math under a 5-year hold.

✗ Operations that need heavy shipping

Most industrial condos share a shipping court with 4–12 other units. If your trucking pattern is dense, owning a standalone building or leasing freestanding is the better fit.
